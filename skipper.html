If you sail with me, you get a skipper who plans ahead, briefs the crew clearly, and keeps the boat and the people safe, so the trip stays enjoyable and not just a test of endurance.

What pressure looks like at sea

The situation

A passage is long and conditions change. Night falls, the weather turns, the crew gets tired, and the next decision can't wait until morning.

The problem

When people are tired and roles are unclear, small misunderstandings grow quickly. A crew that doesn't know the plan hesitates, and a skipper who decides on the fly loses the crew's confidence.

How I work with it

Before departure we agree the plan in a structured briefing: who does what and what we do if conditions change. A clear watch system gives everyone rest, and steady communication on board keeps the crew aligned and confident.
