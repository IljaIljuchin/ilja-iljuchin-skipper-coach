Who this is for

Two kinds of people sail with me: leaders and teams who want to practise leadership in real situations, and owners, crews and charter guests who need someone they can trust at the helm.

In both cases I take responsibility for the boat and the people on board, so you can focus on what you came for: learning, enjoying the sailing, or simply knowing the job is in good hands.

Leadership at sea

For leaders and teams who want to practise leadership in real situations, with a skipper and a coach on board.

What you can develop at sea

On a yacht the situations are real: how you decide, delegate and handle stress shows within hours. A week on board, with a skipper and a coach, is a place to practise and strengthen:

Confidence and self-efficacy

Trust yourself to make the call.

Every day on board brings decisions you can't postpone. Making them, and seeing them work, builds confidence that stays with you.

On board: you plan the next leg, brief the crew and bring the boat safely into a harbour you have never seen.

Leadership, teamwork and communication

Lead a crew, not an org chart.

A small crew in a small space shows quickly how you lead, listen and coordinate, and gives you the chance to practise it.

On board: a sail change at night only works when everyone knows their role and the instructions are clear.

Authority and responsibility

Authority that comes from what you do, not your title.

At sea nobody follows a job title. People follow the person who sets clear expectations, shares the goals and takes responsibility.

On board: you agree on watch roles with people who are senior to you at work.

Work with time and emotions under stress

Stress as an ally, not an enemy.

Pressure at sea is real, and it helps once you know how to work with it: it sharpens your focus and speeds up good decisions.

On board: the wind picks up earlier than forecast, and you decide calmly whether to reef, change course or carry on.

Autonomy and resilience

Keep going when the plan changes.

Weather, fatigue and small breakdowns test how you adapt. You learn to recover quickly and keep a clear head when things don't go to plan.

On board: an engine problem means sailing into port, and the crew looks to you for the new plan.

Self-knowledge and feedback

See how you really lead.

Observation, personality diagnostics and honest debriefs show you how you come across, and what you want to change.

On board: a short debrief every evening on what worked, what didn't and why.

Questions you might have

Do I need a licence or sailing experience to join?

No. I am responsible for the boat and the crew, and everyone helps according to their experience. Beginners learn on the way; more experienced crew take on watches and the helm.

How many miles do we sail?

It depends on the route and the weather. A coastal week is typically around 200 nautical miles (about 40 a day); a week on an offshore passage around 600.

Can you confirm my sea miles?

Yes, on request I confirm the miles, night hours and days at sea you sailed with me. If you collect them for an RYA course or exam, check its current requirements first; we agree on the details before the trip.

What is expected from the crew?

Everyone takes part: watches, sail handling, cooking and keeping the boat tidy. Before departure I brief you on safety, roles and the daily rhythm on board.

What should I wear and bring?

Layers rather than one thick jacket, waterproof jacket and trousers, non-marking deck shoes (sailing boots offshore), a warm hat and gloves for night watches, plus a sun hat, sunglasses and sunscreen. Pack everything in a soft bag, not a hard suitcase. Before every voyage I hold a personal Crew Brief, where I present the passage plan and the conditions for the trip, which every participant agrees to.

Do we sail at night?

On longer passages, yes. A clear watch system means everyone gets proper rest, and nobody is on deck at night without knowing what to do.

What if the weather changes?

Safety comes first. We plan every leg with a plan B, and when conditions change we adjust the route or wait it out, early and calmly.

How do you manage safety and risk?

For every course and development programme on board I work with my own Risk Policy, and every participant goes through it before departure. Together with the Crew Brief it sets out how we assess and manage risks, who does what in an emergency, and when we change the plan. Read the Risk Policy (PDF).

Can you skipper my own boat, or a charter yacht we have booked?

Yes. We agree on the boat, route, dates and crew experience first, and I prepare the passage and the safety briefing with you.

Do you deliver yachts, or join a crew as mate?

Yes. I deliver yachts with or without the owner on board, depending on the conditions, and I also sail as chief mate, the skipper's right hand.

In which language do we sail?

English or Czech, depending on the crew.

How are dates, route and terms arranged?

Individually. After a short call we agree on the boat, destination, timing, crew and terms.

Can I see your sailing CV and log?

Yes. Ask, and I will send my skipper CV and passage history.
